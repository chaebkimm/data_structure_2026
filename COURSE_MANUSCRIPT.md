# Korean Data Structures Course Materials

Students read the textbook. The instructor uses the teaching guide in the same row to ask the first question and walk through the board examples. The required scope for the classes is decided by the [14-Week Delivery Guide](Data_Structures_Course_2026_14_Week_Delivery_Guide.md).

Before starting the course, first read about what data structures and efficiency mean in [How to Store Data Well](module_00_datastructure/textbook.md).

**Modules 12–13 planning status — 2026-11-10.** The teaching guides now
outline Binary Search and BSTs in Week 12 and AVL Rotations and Review in
Week 13. Existing student textbooks, worksheets, and lab materials await
alignment; the links retain their existing locations and content. Use the
revised delivery guide for required scope rather than either old full lab.

The 14 teaching chapters and packages are numbered consecutively from 1 to 14. Module 7 covers acyclic linked lists, shared-pool recycling, and linked stack/queue applications. Module 8 compares array queues, traces tree level order, and uses a direct coordinate array queue for maze BFS in one lesson. Its Korean textbook was frozen on 2026-11-03; the English edition and course materials follow that approved manuscript and the supplied PPTs. Module 10 follows its fixed integer min/max Heap and ten-device shared-on-time lab. Its alert-record Priority Queue and linear baseline remain optional references.

The writing style and the order of concepts follow the [Textbook Style Guide](TEXTBOOK_STYLE_GUIDE.md). Actual testing with beginners will be done using the [Textbook Pilot Guide](textbook_pilot/README.md) once we have participants.
